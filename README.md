# Ozhvaelzeth

> The harbor froze around a flame. The ship inside it continued sailing through winter.

## The Lantern beneath the Ice

Ozhvaelzeth is a harbor reached by walking across a frozen field rather than approaching the sea. Its water lies beneath clear ice. Its ships are visible below the surface, moving without disturbing the pale shapes of waves preserved above them.

A lantern hangs at the end of the quay. Its flame burns downward, casting light into a cabin beneath the ice. Whoever looks into that cabin sees a chair, a folded coat, and a window facing a field that has never frozen.

The harbor keeper carries no lamp of his own. He follows the lantern's shadow along the quay, stopping whenever it points toward a ship that has not yet arrived.

## The Coat with a Warm Collar

A traveler discovered the coat upon the quay, folded as it appeared in the cabin. Its collar was warm, its sleeves filled with frost. Inside one pocket lay a small ship carved from dark horn.

He showed it to the keeper. The man placed the ship against the lantern's glass. Far below, one vessel stopped moving, and the cabin's chair turned toward its window.

The traveler put on the coat. Through the lantern, he saw someone wearing the same garment inside the cabin. That person's face remained hidden by a pale scaled hand resting upon the window.

He removed the coat before the hand withdrew. Its collar retained the warmth of a neck much larger than his own. The carved ship grew heavy enough to leave an impression in the frozen quay.

## A Map Drawn in Unmelted Frost

The keeper charts the harbor on sheets of blue cloth. His lines are made of frost and disappear only when the ships change direction. The map contains no coastline, but several rooms are drawn where islands ought to be.

One room holds a lantern hanging above a small frozen harbor. Another holds an empty chair whose shadow resembles a folded coat. A third remains outlined without an interior, awaiting a ship drawn in dark horn.

An interpreter attempted to connect those rooms with roads. Her pencil produced narrow streams that froze before reaching any doorway.

The keeper asked her which room she had brought ashore. She looked at her hands and found frost beneath every fingernail. Inside it, tiny vessels moved through blue light.

She left the pencil beside the lantern. Its shadow continued drawing a road toward the cabin beneath the quay.

## The Wake of a Sleeping Creature

Below the deepest ship lies a long dark shape. Some witnesses describe a dragon. Others describe the shadow of a vessel with wings folded against its hull.

Its scales reflect rooms containing chairs and lanterns. Its eyes remain closed, but each eyelid shows a moving ship passing beneath a frozen field. The creature breathes slowly, and the vessels change direction whenever its breath reaches the cabin's window.

A sailor lowered a hook through an opening in the ice. He recovered a small horn-colored scale containing the sound of footsteps upon a quay.

The keeper placed it inside the coat's pocket. The carved ship became light again. In the cabin, the pale hand withdrew, revealing a face whose features seemed to have been drawn by frost upon glass.

The lantern burned brighter until everyone looked away.

## The Words upon the Quay's Shadow

**Ozh — vael — zeth — iruun.**

These sounds emerge when the lantern's shadow reaches the impression left by the carved ship. Their letters appear briefly beneath the ice, following the shape of a wake that no vessel has made.

The keeper speaks them only while the coat remains unworn. Each sound warms a different part of its lining, until the collar casts the shadow of a neck stretching toward a window.

The traveler tried repeating the sequence inside the garment. Snow appeared within the lantern's flame. Beneath the ice, the cabin became a frozen field, and the chair stood where the quay should have ended.

He removed the coat. The cabin returned, but its window now reflected a lantern hanging inside a much larger room.

The keeper folded the garment and began charting another outlined room upon his blue cloth.

## The Keeper's Three Directions

> Which ship travels beneath the winter?
>
> The one whose cabin remembers the field above.
>
> What warms the collar of the abandoned coat?
>
> The neck that has not chosen its reflection.
>
> Where should the lantern's shadow lead?
>
> Toward the window that remains open inside the flame.

The interpreter recorded these replies using frost from her hands. The words formed a narrow quay across the cloth, ending at the newly outlined room.

In that room, a small chair became visible. Its shadow wore a coat, though nobody sat upon it.

## The Harbor beyond the Field

The vessels keep moving beneath the ice. Their harbor keeper follows the downward flame's shadow and carries blue cloth wherever an unarrived ship requires a room.

The traveler leaves the coat folded upon the quay. Its collar grows warmer during nights when the long shape beneath the ships turns in its sleep.

Ozhvaelzeth remains frozen around a light that has never reached the sky. Within the cabin, someone waits beside a window, listening for a carved ship to leave a wake across the floor of the larger room.
